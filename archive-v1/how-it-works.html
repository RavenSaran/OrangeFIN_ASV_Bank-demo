Overview / How ASV works

How Automated Signature Verification works

A plain-language walkthrough for project and business stakeholders: what the system does, what it decides, and what it deliberately leaves to people.

End-to-end flow

The same five steps in every use case

1

Receive

Signed form is scanned: payment, FD upliftment or transfer.

2

Extract

Handwriting is located and cropped. Overlap or poor quality is flagged.

3

Look up

The verified specimen of the required signatory is fetched, using the account's signing mandate.

4

Compare

A similarity score from 0 to 100 is produced for each signature.

5

Route

Pass continues to normal approval. Anything else goes to a reviewer.

What happens to the result

Three possible outcomes

Likely match

Score at or above 70, mandate met

Continues through the bank's normal approval workflow. Staff can still approve, recheck or reject.

Suspicious

Score below 70, or cannot compare

Flagged for manual review. Includes overlapping writing, cut-off signatures or very faint scans. A low score does not prove forgery.

Mandate not met

Signatures genuine, rule not satisfied

For example one signature where the account needs two, or the same person signing twice on an “any 2 of 3” rule.

Governance

Important limits, stated openly

Reference quality comes first

ASV is only as good as the specimen on record. Each signature must be compared with the specific signatory the mandate requires, not any signature on the account.

Genuine signatures vary

Writing conditions, age, speed and scan quality all change a signature. That is why the result is a score for review, not proof.

Image-only comparison

Scanned documents carry no pen pressure, signing speed or stroke order. Those signals are only available if captured at signing time.

Humans stay in control

High-value instructions are never authorised on a score alone. Thresholds and policy are set by the bank after model validation.
